Advice

Understanding your treatment

Guides written by our team so you know what to expect and how to look after your teeth.

Oral hygiene

Regular, thorough brushing removes dental plaque, the main cause of tooth decay and gum disease.

Twice a day, two minutes

Brush your teeth morning and evening for two minutes. The evening brush is the most important.

The toothbrush

Choose a soft brush with a small head, manual or electric. Replace it every three months, or sooner if the bristles splay.

Toothpaste

Use a fluoride toothpaste: 1,450 ppm fluoride for adults. For children, the strength depends on age: ask us. After brushing, spit out without rinsing too much.

Between the teeth

Morning and evening, also clean between your teeth with floss or interdental brushes.

Check-ups at the practice

A check-up at least once a year catches problems early and allows a scale and polish if needed.

Sources: UFSBD (French Union for Oral Health), public advice · HAS (French National Authority for Health), strategies to prevent tooth decay · Today’s RDH, toothbrushing methods (modified Stillman)

Tooth decay

Tooth decay is caused by bacteria in dental plaque, which turn sugars into acids. These acids attack the enamel, then the dentine.

What causes it

Poor brushing, snacking and sugary or acidic drinks throughout the day, smoking, a dry mouth.

Where it starts

In the grooves of the teeth, between the teeth and near the gum. In children, fissure sealants protect the molars.

The signs

Early decay does not hurt. Sensitivity to cold or sweet things is a reason to see us. Don’t wait for pain: the earlier it is treated, the simpler the treatment.

Treatment

The decayed part is removed, under local anaesthetic if needed, then the tooth is rebuilt with a tooth-coloured composite filling. If the tooth is badly damaged, a ceramic inlay or onlay strengthens it. If the infection has reached the nerve, root canal treatment is needed (see root canal treatment).

Keeping the tooth alive

When decay is deep but the pulp is still healthy, we try to avoid root canal treatment. After removing the decay, we place Biodentine, a bioactive calcium silicate cement, at the bottom of the cavity. It helps a bridge of dentine to form that protects the pulp: the tooth stays alive. This is only possible if the pulp is not too badly affected; the decision is made after examination and sensitivity tests.

Sources: HAS, caries risk and fissure sealants · HAS (French National Authority for Health), strategies to prevent tooth decay · European Society of Endodontology (ESE), deep caries and the exposed pulp, 2019

Root canal treatment

Endodontics treats the inside of the tooth: the pulp, often called the nerve, and the root canals. The aim is to save your tooth, even when the pulp is affected.

When is it needed?

When the pulp is irreversibly inflamed (spontaneous pain, pain that wakes you at night, lasting sensitivity to heat), when it is necrotic, in case of an abscess, or after a blow to the tooth.

Root canal treatment

Under local anaesthetic, the pulp is removed completely. The canals are cleaned, disinfected and shaped, then sealed. An X-ray checks the result. Treatment takes one or two appointments depending on the case.

After treatment

Some discomfort when chewing for a few days is normal. A root-treated tooth is more fragile: it must then be restored with a filling, an onlay or a crown, depending on how much is left.

Retreatment

If a root-treated tooth becomes infected again, the treatment can often be redone: this is called endodontic retreatment.

A living tooth is always better

Whenever possible, root canal treatment is avoided: with deep decay and a pulp that is still healthy, Biodentine can keep the tooth alive (see the guide on tooth decay).

Crowns, bridges and dentures

The crown

It covers and protects a badly damaged or root-treated tooth. It can also be fitted on an implant.

Inlays and onlays

A custom-made ceramic piece bonded to the tooth to rebuild the missing part of a damaged tooth. Only the affected area is removed, so it preserves more of the tooth than a crown.

The bridge

It replaces one or two missing teeth by resting on the neighbouring teeth, which must be prepared. It is fixed.

Removable dentures

Partial or complete, it replaces several teeth and is taken out for cleaning. It can be stabilised with implants.

Dental implants

An implant is an artificial titanium root placed in the bone to replace a missing tooth. It then holds a crown.

Advantages

It does not affect the neighbouring teeth, it is fixed, it feels close to a natural tooth, and it limits bone loss after an extraction.

How it works

An assessment with X-rays checks that there is enough bone. The implant is placed under local anaesthetic. It then bonds with the bone over a few weeks to a few months, before the final crown is fitted.

Our implants

We use IDI implants, designed and made in France since 1987, in Montreuil. Each implant is traceable from manufacture to placement.

Good to know

Implants are not placed before growth is complete. Smoking and some medical conditions can affect healing: mention them at your assessment.

Sources: IDI, implant manufacturer · EFP, preventing peri-implantitis · SFPIO (French Society of Periodontology and Oral Implantology)

Bone and sinus grafts

An implant needs enough bone. After an extraction, or when the bone is too thin, it can be preserved or rebuilt before the implant is placed.

Socket preservation

After an extraction, the bone around the root shrinks and the gum sinks. Filling the socket straight away with a bone substitute, protected by a membrane, limits this loss and prepares for the implant 4 to 6 months later.

Onlay bone graft

When the ridge is too thin to hold an implant, it is thickened by adding bone, your own or a substitute, to its side, protected by a membrane. The graft heals in 4 to 6 months.

Lateral sinus lift

At the back of the upper jaw, the sinus sometimes reaches so low that only a few millimetres of bone remain. Through a small window on the cheek side, its thin membrane is lifted and graft material is placed underneath. The implant is placed after 6 to 9 months, or on the same day if enough bone remains to stabilise it. With 5 mm of bone or more, the sinus can often be lifted directly through the implant site (crestal approach).

After the procedure

Swelling and bruising are possible for a few days: ice for 48 hours, soft food, no smoking, which slows bone formation. After a sinus lift: do not blow your nose for 2 to 3 weeks, sneeze with your mouth open, no flying or diving without our agreement. Call the practice if you have a fever, a nasal discharge or swelling that gets worse.

Sources: EFP, JCP Digest 99, alveolar ridge preservation (2022) · ITI, consensus on bone augmentation · Thomas and Bidra, Compendium, 2018

Gum care

Healthy gums are pink and firm and do not bleed when you brush.

Warning signs

Bleeding, swollen or red gums, persistent bad breath, loose teeth or receding gums: see us without delay.

The cause

Plaque and tartar build up under the gum and inflame it: this is gingivitis. Without treatment, the inflammation can reach the bone holding the teeth: this is periodontitis. Smoking and diabetes increase the risk.

Treatment

Deep cleaning (scaling and root planing), learning the right brushing technique with interdental brushes, then regular follow-up. In some cases, gum surgery is needed.

Sources: SFPIO (French Society of Periodontology and Oral Implantology) · UFSBD, gum disease leaflet

Read the full guide →
Teeth whitening

Whitening lightens the natural shade of teeth that have been dulled by age, coffee, tea or tobacco.

First of all

An examination checks for decay and gum problems. Whitening is for adults only and does not work on crowns or composite fillings.

How it works

Depending on your case, with custom trays worn at home for a few weeks, or in a session at the practice. Temporary sensitivity is possible.

Making it last

Good brushing, and less coffee, tea and tobacco.

Dental emergencies

Pain, broken tooth, abscess, a fall: call the practice, we answer 24/7.

Sundays and public holidays

Call 15 (French emergency medical line), who will direct you to the on-call dentist.

Call 15 immediately

In case of major facial swelling, fever, difficulty swallowing or breathing, or a violent blow.

Knocked-out adult tooth

Hold it by the crown without touching the root, rinse it briefly if it is dirty, keep it in milk or in your saliva, and see a dentist within the hour.
