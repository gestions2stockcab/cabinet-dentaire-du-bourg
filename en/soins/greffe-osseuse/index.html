Bone grafts and sinus lifts

Socket preservation, onlay bone grafts and lateral sinus lifts, with diagrams: preparing the bone before an implant, at Cabinet Dentaire du Bourg, Savigny-le-Temple.

An implant needs enough bone. After an extraction, or when the bone is too thin, it can be preserved or rebuilt before the implant is placed.

Socket preservation

After an extraction, the bone around the root shrinks and the gum sinks. Filling the socket straight away with a bone substitute, protected by a membrane, limits this loss and prepares for the implant 4 to 6 months later.

Onlay bone graft

When the ridge is too thin to hold an implant, it is thickened by adding bone, your own or a substitute, to its side, protected by a membrane. The graft heals in 4 to 6 months.

Lateral window sinus lift

At the back of the upper jaw, the sinus sometimes reaches so low that only a few millimetres of bone remain. Through a small window on the cheek side, its membrane (the Schneiderian membrane) is lifted and graft material is placed underneath. The implant is placed after 6 to 9 months, or on the same day if enough bone remains to stabilise it. With 5 mm of bone or more, the sinus can often be lifted directly through the implant site (crestal approach).

After the procedure

Swelling and bruising are possible for a few days: ice for 48 hours, soft food, no smoking, which slows bone formation. After a sinus lift: do not blow your nose for 2 to 3 weeks, sneeze with your mouth open, no flying or diving without our agreement. Call the practice if you have a fever, a nasal discharge or swelling that gets worse.
